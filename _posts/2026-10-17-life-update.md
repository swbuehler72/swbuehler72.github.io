## Seasons

The summer rainy season is officially over and we're now dealing with our first cold front of the fall/winter/spring dry season. The pattern is fairly straightforward for the next several months:

- A cold front passes through with heavy thunderstorms and rain;

- Temperatures drop _extremely_ low (for Florida) for a couple of days&mdash;often into the 30s and 40s °F at night and 50s to 60s during the day;

- Conditions become postcard perfect (70s °F) until the next cold front arrives.

It's an ideal time to get back outside and reestablish my daily walking/activity habit until summer sets back in and we go back into hibernation until mid-Fall again. However, the strong _El Niño_ phenomenon over the Pacific is predicted to bring a wetter-than-usual winter to the Florida peninsula. An _El Niño_ occurs when water temperatures in the Pacific are warmer than normal; a _La Niña_ occurs when they are colder than normal. The other effect is that there have been no hurricanes originating from the Atlantic Ocean or Caribbean Sea thus far in the hurricane season, and the end of the season on November 30<sup>th</sup> may very well pass with zero for the first time since satellites have tracked the tropics. (We did have one make landfall out of the Gulf of <del>Mexico</del> America onto the Florida panhandle, where my folks would normally be snowbirding during January and February before advancing age made that annual trip impractical.)

However, Parkinson's, advancing age and its consequences make that no longer practical, so this past year I instead jetted up to them in rural Michigan over the same period (Breeze has direct flights between Orlando and Lansing on Wednesdays and Saturdays, so up on the weekend and back in the middle of the week).

I'll be flying up to spend Thanksgiving week in rural Michigan, and hopefully both sisters will be able to make the trip over from New York to spend Thanksgiving together for the first time in over thirty years.
